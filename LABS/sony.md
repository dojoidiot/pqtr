How this maps to “Opposing-Colour / Colour-Matrix / Channel Gain” Thinking

Because Sony gives you per-channel “colour depth” adjustments + hue shifts + overall saturation/hue-phase control, you can approximate manually what you might do in post-processing (or in theory with channel gain or Lab-axis tweaks).

Boosting certain channels — e.g. increasing the “Red” channel colour depth will make reds more saturated, boosting “Green” does likewise for greens. This is somewhat analogous to increasing channel gain in an RGB curve.

Shifting hue globally (Colour Phase) — allows “push” toward warmer (reds/yellows) or cooler (greens/blues) overall, similar in effect to a global colour-balance / temperature tint.

Overall saturation control — like adjusting saturation or vibrance in post, affecting all colours.

Different colour matrices / colour modes — Sony’s built-in matrices may remap hues and saturation differently (some more muted, some richer), similar to selecting a different colour space or tone curve. For example, a “Cinema” colour matrix might produce more film-like, subtle saturation, while a “Standard/Pro” matrix might yield more vivid colours.

Gamma / contrast / tonal curve adjustments — by changing gamma curve or shadow/highlight handling, Sony controls how brightness interacts with colour; that affects how “rich” or “flat” colours appear, especially in shadows/highlights — analogous to adjusting contrast before/after colour adjustment.

Hence, the principle of “opposing-colour axes” and “channel-gain/colour-matrix manipulation” you described can be partially implemented in camera using Picture Profile — with controls that approximate (in a more limited and pre-defined way) what you might do with a full editing suite.

Sony’s in-camera tools give you good “global style” control — letting you push colour, saturation, hue-balance and contrast without external editing.

If you want to quickly give a look (e.g. warm, cinematic, saturated, moody) straight out of camera — Picture Profile or Creative Look are very useful.

If you want precise, channel-by-channel control (e.g. boost red + green moderately but keep blue neutral for a subtle warm vibe without over-saturation) — that’s more limited in-camera; you’d probably do better in post-processing the RAW.

And if you shoot video and want colour/contrast baked in (especially before compression), Picture Profile is useful because adjustments apply before compression — preserving best possible detail.
