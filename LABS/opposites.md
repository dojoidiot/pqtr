Here’s how to think of it:

🧭 Step Logic (Global Colour Rule)
Step	What You See	Axis	Rule	Effect if Boosted
1	Blue windows	B↔Y	Boost blue = cooler; cut blue = warmer	Keep blues rich but not cyan
2	Red flowers	R↔G	Boost red, balance green	Reds pop, leaves stay natural
3	White walls	Neutral (balance point)	Keep R,G,B in equilibrium	Prevent tint drift
4	Overall mix	Combine B–Y and R–G axes	Adjust warmth or coolness based on mood	Scene feels morning (warm) or noon (cool)
🎨 Heuristic Matrix for This Scene
Goal	R	G	B	Result
Natural daylight (balanced)	0	0	0	True whites; neutral tone
Warm tone (sunset feel)	+	+	−	Yellow warmth; blue windows soften
Cool tone (architectural crisp)	−	0	+	Blue pop; red flowers less dominant
Pop contrast (colourful)	↑contrast on R–G & B–Y	—	—	Reds, greens, blues stand out; walls stay white
Artistic warm–cool split	+R +B	−G	—	Reds and blues rich; teal-ish leaves
⚙️ Rule of Thumb

Keep neutrals neutral (white walls define balance).
Boost opposite pairs equally to preserve realism.
Push contrast on axes, not global saturation, if you want pop without tint.

💡Example in practice

If you slightly raise Red and Blue (the warm + cool ends) while gently lowering Green, you get a vivid, cinematic look — red flowers glow, blue glass stays intense, white walls remain near-neutral.
If instead you raise Green too much, the flowers go dull and the walls drift yellow.
